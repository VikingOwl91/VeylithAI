## 2. Router-Aux audit

The per-layer value is the Switch-style normalized load-balancing loss

`aux_layer = E * sum_e (assignment_fraction_e * mean_router_prob_e)`

with `E = 8`, top-2 assignment, full-softmax probabilities. For balanced
routing, both distributions are uniform and `aux_layer = 1.0`. The model
returns the **sum over its 7 MoE layers**, so a balanced model reports
`aux_loss = 7.0`. The GPU smoke's 7.44-9.62 therefore means per-layer
1.06-1.37, not a broken normalization. The first real run logged per-layer
1.067 at step 10 and 1.003 at step 100; the sum fell to 7.019.

The coefficient 0.01 is applied to the sum, i.e. 0.01 per MoE layer. The
weighted contribution was 0.0747 nats at step 10 and 0.0702 at step 100,
against a main CE of 8.14 / 7.38 (about 0.9 %). The objective is unchanged.
The aux gradient reaches the router gate; gradient accumulation weights the
aux by valid tokens, so the effective loss is the token-weighted mean (no
scaling change). `aux_loss_per_layer` and the trainer's `aux_weighted` /
`aux_per_layer` fields were added as diagnostics, with a CPU regression test
that a uniform router gives exactly 1.0 per layer and a sum equal to the
number of MoE layers. No objective correction was required.

## 3. Run result

- 100 optimizer updates completed; 3,270,400 CE tokens; wall 134.2 s
  (2.2 min), far under the 20-minute limit.
- Training loss (logged every 10 updates): 8.2145 at step 10 to 7.4531 at
  step 100; main CE 8.1398 to 7.3829; grad norm 1.201 to 0.248.
- Last step: 0.895 s, 36,536 tokens/s, 1.767 GB allocated / 4.121 GB
  reserved VRAM (peak).
- No NaN/Inf, no OOM, no GPU fault, no step over 120 s. The test split was
  not used.

## 5. Router

All 8 experts were used in every MoE layer at the initial and final
validation; no unused experts. Mean normalized entropy 0.960 -> 0.968;
per-layer final entropy 0.919-0.981 in validation and about 0.994 in the
last training window. Layer 5 has the lowest entropy (0.919) with one
expert at 2.8 %, but no collapse. The short random/synthetic smoke's low
entropy at microbatch 1 did not persist.

## 7. Comparison with the smoke and analytical estimates

- Smoke MB8 one microbatch: 0.150 s / 27,345 tokens/s. Real update is 8
  accumulation microbatches: 0.895 s / 36,536 tokens/s; consistent
  (about 0.11 s per microbatch at the real batch mix, better than the random
  smoke's 0.15 s).
- VRAM: 1.77 GB allocated / 4.12 GB reserved peak, inside the analytical
  4.04 GB estimate (which included 1.9 GB reserve and 12 % margin).
- Throughput: 36.5k tokens/s, so one 95.95M-token pass would take about
  44 minutes, not the 0.97 h extrapolated from the single-microbatch smoke.

## 8. Recommendation

1. The infrastructure works on real data: stable loss, routing, VRAM,
   checkpoint load and selection. No fault.
2. Next step: a longer bounded run (for example 2,000-5,000 updates) with
   step-suffixed checkpoints and a real resume test, still well below a full
   pass, and with the same no-retry/no-dense-fallback discipline.
3. The 3.27M-token slice is too small for a quality claim. A meaningful 0.1B
   run needs the 500M-1B-token data budget and a separately approved plan.
4. Keep validation-only selection and the per-source reporting.
5. Do not start the next phase without explicit authorization.
