# v3.1-PERF-4A host overhead and launch amortization audit

Status 2026-10-27. Read-only audit. No GPU execution, no training, no code change.

**Headline: the update is limited by the host, not by the device, at the 6 percent
level. And 28 percent of all device kernels compute nothing: they are the NaN
prefills that deterministic mode adds to every fresh allocation.**

The three measured anchors:

| quantity | value | source |
|---|---:|---|
| device kernels per update | 40,975 | PERF-3D trace a0/b0, 8 updates each |
| device busy per update | 715 ms | sum of kernel durations, same traces |
| wall per update | 780.45 ms (production loop, 150 updates) | PERF-1 |
| wall per update | 758.9 ms (harness, 40 updates) | PERF-3D |
| device idle per update | 47 to 53 ms | wall minus device busy |
| deterministic NaN prefills | 11,545 kernels, 43.8 ms | PERF-3D trace, all four functor types |

Because the wall exceeds the device busy time, host-side work is on the critical
path. That single fact explains the PERF-3D result: a change that removed 5.3 ms
of device work and added 12.1 ms of host work made the update 6.6 ms slower.
It also retires the PERF-3A plan of fusing more device-side elementwise work:
those candidates cannot appear in wall time while the host is the limiter.

**Selected PERF-4B experiment: remove the deterministic NaN prefill, change
nothing else.** It is the largest measured, purely wasteful item in the update,
it is one flag, and it is reversible. Expected wall change: -30 to -60 ms
(4 to 8 percent), bounded above by -91 ms. This is a hypothesis; Section 9
defines the gates that will test it.

## 3. Launch accounting

From the two PERF-3D baseline traces, 8 profiled updates each, kernel events
only:

| family | kernels/update | share | device ms/update | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| elementwise | 19,430 | 47.4% | 286.3 | 40.0% |
| GEMM (Tensile) | 5,064 | 12.4% | 246.3 | 34.4% |
| other (attention fwd, copies, scans) | 1,915 | 4.7% | 82.7 | 11.5% |
| **deterministic NaN prefill** | **11,545** | **28.2%** | **43.8** | **6.1%** |
| reductions | 1,428 | 3.5% | 24.7 | 3.4% |
| silu backward | 456 | 1.1% | 14.3 | 2.0% |
| dropout and RNG | 520 | 1.3% | 8.5 | 1.2% |
| silu forward | 456 | 1.1% | 7.0 | 1.0% |
| copy and cast | 161 | 0.4% | 2.6 | 0.4% |
| **total** | **40,975** | | **716.0** | |

Prefill split by element type: float 5,006 kernels (21.4 ms), bfloat16 4,795
(17.8 ms), int64 1,224 (2.5 ms), bool 520 (2.1 ms).

The prefill is not model code. `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`
(`train.py:140-142`) enables `fill_uninitialized_memory`, and every `empty` or
`empty_like` then becomes an allocation plus a NaN fill. Mechanism proven on
device in PERF-3D: with deterministic mode off, zero fills; on, two per call;
fill flag off, zero again. Explicit zero-initialisation in the model
(`model.py:143` once per MoE layer, `model.py:214` once per forward) accounts for
about 64 of the 11,545, so at least 99 percent is prefill.

The 11,545 fills cost 3.8 us each, which is launch latency, not bandwidth: the
buffers are small enough to be overwritten in L2 before eviction.

There are 159,094 recorded aten calls per update against 40,975 kernels: the
dispatch layer sees roughly four calls per kernel. `aten::as_strided` alone is
15,156 per update, `aten::transpose` 9,992, `aten::t` 8,456, `aten::to` 7,199.
Most of these are zero-cost view metadata, but each is a Python-level call.

Uninstrumented process CPU per update is 1.506 s (PERF-3D timing runs), against
2.03 s instrumented, so the profiler inflates host time by about 35 percent. The
launch-cost figures above are therefore upper bounds on the uninstrumented cost,
and the 5.8 us per launch should not be used as a production number.

## 5. Where the wall time goes

Measured:

- Device busy: 715 ms. Wall: 759 ms (harness) and 780 ms (production loop).
- Device idle: 47 to 53 ms, or 6 to 7 percent of the update.
- GPU busy percentage from telemetry during PERF-1: 95.1 percent, which is the
  same statement from the other side.
- Loader: 13.7 ms per window, on the main thread, outside `step_seconds`.
- PERF-3D pass-through: +12.1 ms of host cost produced +6.6 ms of wall (54
  percent).

Derived:

```
wall ~ max(host_critical_path, device_busy) + serialization residual
```

With device at 715 ms and wall at 759 to 780 ms, the host critical path is the
binding constraint today. Two consequences follow, and PERF-3D already confirmed
the first:

1. A change that removes device work and adds host work loses wall time. PERF-3D:
   -5.3 ms device, +12.1 ms host, +6.6 ms wall.
2. A change that removes device work without touching the host cannot help until
   the host drops below the device time. The PERF-3A candidates P2 (MoE weighted
   aggregation, about 15 ms) and P3 (SwiGLU, measured 5.3 ms) are both in this
   category. Neither should be built.

Not established: how much of the 47 to 53 ms of idle is barrier-induced versus
host-work-induced. That is what PERF-4B will discriminate, because removing the
prefill reduces both host work and device work at once.

## 6. Strategy evaluation

Numerical admissibility is judged against the standing contract: bitwise
identity with the deterministic baseline from PERF-3B and PERF-3D, no tolerance
substitution.

| strategy | host reduction | device effect | numerical boundaries | autograd | routing and graph breaks | dropout RNG | 16 GB VRAM | ROCm gfx1201 | complexity | reversible | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. scoped `torch.compile`/Inductor** | high in principle: elementwise chains and the 8-expert Python loop can be fused, cutting dispatches | unknown; Inductor may also pick different GEMM schedules | **bad**: fuses elementwise chains across BF16 rounding points, exactly the PERF-3B mechanism | automatic, but AOTAutograd may reorder accumulation | MoE loop is static (dense), so graph capture is plausible; view/`topk`/boolean-mask patterns risk breaks | Inductor emits its own Philox offsets; equality with baseline unproven | +1 to +2 GB for compiled artifacts and extra buffers; must stay under 6 GiB guard | Inductor on gfx1201 unproven in this build; no local evidence | medium-high | recompile off, revert import | predicted bitwise failure |
| **B. HIP graph capture and replay** | highest: 40,975 launches plus 159k dispatches collapse to one replay | neutral; same kernels replay | values identical only if RNG and pointers match | static graph must cover backward; DDP-style capture not needed here | dense MoE is static, so shapes are stable; `bool(mask.any())` style checks are sparse-only | **blocked**: capture freezes dropout seed and offset into kernel arguments, so every replay reuses one mask set and diverges from the baseline at update 2 | needs a private pool sized to peak (5.6 GiB reserved today, plus capture copies); tight on 16 GB | `torch.cuda.CUDAGraph` present in this build; HIP replay unexercised here | high: 89 in-region barriers must be removed or moved outside capture, allocation must be static | capture behind a flag; baseline path untouched | not admissible under the current contract |
| **C. larger fused execution regions** | negative: measured +26.5 us per call in PERF-3D | small | bad for the same rounding reason | custom Function per region | n/a | unaffected if outside dropout | neutral | Triton 3.8 works | medium | yes | rejected on measurement |
| **D. batched or grouped expert execution** | some: 1,344 expert GEMMs per update become 168 batched calls | large: dense fallback computes 8 experts where 2 are used, so 4x expert FLOPs are wasted | **bad**: gathering tokens per expert changes GEMM shapes and row order, so results are not bitwise comparable | needs a custom scatter/gather backward | routing-dependent; discrete token counts break static shapes | unaffected | grouped buffers add a few hundred MB | rocBLAS/Tensile batched GEMM available but unverified for this shape set | high | yes | blocked numerically; also a device-side win in a host-bound regime |
| **E. reduced Python dispatch and allocation** | high and direct: fewer launches, fewer allocations, fewer host reads | small but positive: removing prefills also removes 43.8 ms of device time | none: the same kernels run on the same values | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged | neutral | no new dependency | low | one flag or one loop edit | admissible |

Notes on the two that look most attractive on paper:

- B is the only strategy with enough headroom to make the loop properly
  device-bound, but it is blocked by dropout, not by ROCm. A captured graph
  freezes the Philox seed and offset into the kernel arguments, so every replay
  would reuse the same dropout masks. The baseline draws fresh masks per
  microbatch and per update. Matching that would require re-capturing per update
  (which defeats the purpose) or changing the RNG contract (which the standing
  policy does not allow). Any future graph work must first answer the RNG
  question, not the capture question.
- A is the only strategy that reduces both host and device cost together, which
  is what a host-bound loop needs. It is ranked third only because its
  probability of passing a bitwise gate is low: Inductor fuses elementwise
  chains, and PERF-3B proved that moving a BF16 rounding point changes gradients.

| 3 | A, scoped Inductor | weak; no local evidence on ROCm | unknown, possibly -100 ms | medium | **high**, rounding boundaries | +1 to +2 GB | possible behind a flag |
| 4 | D, batched experts | structural analysis only | large device win, but device wins do not reach wall today | high | **blocked**, GEMM shapes | moderate | no |
| 5 | C, more fused regions | **negative**: measured host regression | none | medium | high | none | no |

Numbers in rank 1 are partly derived: 43.8 ms of device time is measured, the
host share is inferred from launch counts, and the wall effect is a hypothesis
bounded by the 47 to 53 ms of idle.

The reason E ranks first is not that it is clever. It is that the update pays
11,545 kernel launches per update to write NaN into memory that is about to be
overwritten, in a loop where the host is already the limiter.

## 8. Why this is the right next experiment rather than graph capture

Graph capture would remove far more host work, but it cannot pass the current
numerical contract because of dropout, and it would require removing all 89
in-region barriers first. The prefill removal needs neither. It is also a
prerequisite either way: a captured graph would still capture the fills unless
they are removed, and those 11,545 launches per update would remain inside the
replay.

So the order is forced: remove the waste that is admissible now, re-measure where
the limiter sits, and only then decide whether the remaining host cost justifies
the restructuring that graphs require.

## 9. PERF-4B design (not authorized here)

**Single variable: deterministic NaN prefill off. Everything else identical.**

### Scope

- New experimental files only: `veylith/v3/perf/v31p4b_nofill.py` (shim and
  gate) and `veylith/v3/perf/v31p4b_harness.py`.
- The shim wraps `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms` so that when the training
  loop enables deterministic mode, the prefill flag is turned off immediately
  afterwards. Kernel determinism stays on.
- Production files untouched: `train.py`, `model.py`, the optimizer, the
  schedule, the checkpoint format and the data pipeline.

### Correctness tests

1. Flag-state test: after `use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`, kernel
   determinism is on and `fill_uninitialized_memory` is off; both are restored
   when the shim is not used.
2. Prefill-count test: with the flag off, fill kernels drop from about 11,545 to
   the explicit-zero baseline of about 64 per update. Measured by kernel name in
   a trace, not by inference.
3. **Uninitialized-content invariance test.** Run the two-update gate twice with
   the flag off, once after priming the allocator with a 0xAA byte pattern and
   once with 0x55, and require bitwise identical results. This is the test that
   answers the real risk of disabling the safety fill: it proves no result
   depends on uninitialized memory for these steps.

### Two-update deterministic gate

Same protocol as PERF-3D: baseline twice, variant once, fresh processes from the
verified step-0 state, comparing logits, main CE, auxiliary loss, router digests,
gradient digests, weight digests, optimizer-state digests and learning rate.
Require zero mismatched fields and a deterministic baseline.

### Timing protocol

- Gate first, then timing. No timing if the gate fails.
- Production path, not a reimplementation: drive `training.train.train` with
  `stop_after=60`, `log_every=1`, `eval_interval=1000`, as PERF-1 did, so the
  measured `step_seconds` is the same metric as PERF-1's 780.5 ms.
- Four interleaved arms: baseline, no-prefill, no-prefill, baseline, 60 updates
  each after warmup. Report per-update medians, trimmed means and CE targets/s.
- One profiled arm per condition to confirm kernel counts and the launch delta,
  using the PERF-1 attribution method.
- Sampler running throughout for power and busy percent.

### Memory and power checks

Peak allocated and reserved must not exceed the PERF-1 values (5.568 GiB
allocated, 6.107 GiB reserved) and must stay under the configured guard; mean and
peak package power reported but not used as a decision input on their own.

### Abort conditions

- Any field mismatch in the two-update gate, or a non-deterministic baseline.
- Any non-finite loss, gradient or weight.
- Fill kernels not reduced to the explicit-zero baseline.
- Wall time not improved by at least 10 ms, which would falsify the host-bound
  model rather than the change, and must trigger a re-derivation instead of a
  larger run.
- GPU fault, environment drift, or peak VRAM above the guard.

### Rollback

Delete the two experimental files. Nothing in the production path is modified, so
rollback is file removal and a checkout of nothing else.

### Prediction, stated so it can be wrong

| quantity | baseline | predicted no-prefill |
|---|---:|---:|
| fill kernels/update | 11,545 | ~64 |
| device ms/update | 715 | 671 |
| wall ms/update | 780 | 720 to 750 |
| CE targets/s | 41,773 | 43,500 to 45,400 |

If the wall does not move, the host-bound model is wrong and the next step is a
barrier study, not a larger experiment.

## 10. Unknowns and risks

- Barrier cost is bounded but not attributed: 47 to 53 ms of device idle is the
  total envelope for 89 host reads plus whatever host work exceeds the device.
- The instrumented host table inflates every row by about 35 percent; the shares
  are usable, the absolute microseconds are not.
- Removing the prefill trades a loud NaN signal for silence if a latent
  uninitialized read exists. The invariance test in PERF-4B is designed to catch
  that for the gated steps; it cannot prove it for every future step. This is a
  judgement for the maintainer, not a numerical result.
- Whether HIP graph capture would even replay correctly on gfx1201 is unproven;
  the dropout objection makes it moot until the RNG contract is revisited.
- Inductor's bitwise behaviour on this build is untested; the prediction of
  failure is reasoned from PERF-3B, not measured.
- The dense MoE fallback wastes 4x expert FLOPs. That is the largest structural
  inefficiency in the model, and it stays blocked by the unresolved sparse fault
  and by GEMM-shape numerics.
