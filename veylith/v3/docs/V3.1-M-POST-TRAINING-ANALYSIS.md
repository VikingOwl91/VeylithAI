# v3.1-M post-training analysis of the v3.1-L run

Status 2026-10-27. CPU-only, read-only analysis of
`~/veylith-v31l-dense-20261027`: 500 optimizer updates, 16,352,000 CE targets,
Dense-Combined MoE, BF16, seed 0.

Every number below is either **measured** (read from the run artifacts or
produced by the bounded CPU inference described in section 2), **calculated**
(arithmetic over measured values, with the formula stated) or **hypothesis**
(explicitly labelled). Nothing here is a projection of a future loss curve.

No GPU work, no optimizer update, no weight change and no write to the
experiment directory occurred. The test split was not read.

### 3.5 Convergence, plateau, instability, overfitting

**Measured:** validation improves at every checkpoint; the per-update rate
falls monotonically across the three intervals. Training loss falls from 9.12
to about 5.5 with a transient rise in the router auxiliary term around updates
5 to 8. No NaN, Inf or loss spike after warmup. Update time is stable after
the first update (median 0.745 s).

**Calculated:** the last interval still yields 0.2035 macro CE per 100 updates,
about 27 percent of the whole-run average rate (0.7433). That is decelerating
improvement, not a measured plateau.

**Assessment:**

- Convergence: not reached in the measured window.
- Plateau: no, the curve is still decreasing at step 500.
- Instability: a bounded early transient (gradient clipping at updates 1 to
  10, router entropy dip to 0.817 around update 7, recovery above 0.96 by
  update 30). After that the run is stable.
- Overfitting: no evidence. Validation CE is below the training microbatch CE
  at every shared step (-0.0075 at 100, -0.0407 at 250, -0.1011 at 500), and
  only 17 percent of one epoch was seen. That comparison is confounded by
  dropout being active during training and disabled during evaluation, and by
  the training value being one 64-sequence batch, so it cannot be used as a
  quantitative generalization gap. It only rules out a large overfitting
  signature.
- Continuing improvement: yes, over the measured window, at a falling rate.

## 4. Router analysis

### 4.1 What the logged entropy measures

`_router_summary` computes entropy over the top-2 assignment counts, normalized
by `log(8)`. Two assignments per token are counted. This is a load-balance
measure, not a measure of how confident the softmax is. The bounded inference
in section 5 measures the softmax distribution separately.

### 4.2 Per-layer balance over training

Validation-split assignment entropy and share spread:

| step | mean entropy | min share | max share | max CV | max(max/min) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.960173 | 4.21 % | 30.16 % | 0.589 | 7.16 |
| 100 | 0.965379 | 1.92 % | 28.38 % | 0.597 | 14.78 |
| 250 | 0.998867 | 10.58 % | 14.74 % | 0.086 | 1.31 |
| 500 | 0.999278 | 10.62 % | 14.09 % | 0.073 | 1.27 |

At step 500 every layer is within 0.9987 to 0.9996 entropy, shares within
10.62 to 14.09 percent (a perfectly uniform share is 12.5 percent), and the
coefficient of variation is at most 0.073. All 8 experts are used in all 7 MoE
layers; no expert is unused at any validation.

The training log shows a transient imbalance: mean entropy 0.9644 at update 1,
0.8861 at update 5, 0.8633 at update 10, then 0.9654 at 30 and 0.9992 at 250.
Per-layer entropy at update 5 ranged from 0.7897 to 0.9619. This is the warmup
phase with the highest learning rates; the auxiliary term pulls the load back
to balance within about 20 updates.

Note on the final value: 0.999278 is very close to the uniform ceiling of 1.0.
It is the expected result of minimizing the auxiliary loss, not independent
evidence of useful specialization.

### 4.3 Is routing confident at the token level?

Measured on 240 bounded validation sequences. Normalized softmax entropy (over
8 experts) and mean top probability, averaged per layer and source:

| model | softmax entropy | mean max probability | top-1 to top-2 margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| init (step 0) | 0.949 to 0.966 | 0.211 to 0.235 | 0.043 to 0.063 |
| step 500 | 0.612 to 0.867 | 0.355 to 0.581 | 0.174 to 0.429 |

At initialization the gate is nearly uniform and low-confidence. At step 500
the router is substantially sharper, most strongly in layer 1 (entropy 0.612
to 0.623, max probability 0.572 to 0.581) and least in layer 7 (0.865 to
0.867). So the near-uniform assignment distribution is not a near-uniform
softmax: many tokens are routed decisively, and their choices are spread
almost evenly across experts.

## 5. Expert specialization

Three claims are distinguished throughout: balanced utilization, distinct
routing behavior, and learned specialization.

### 5.1 Router weight evolution

The gate is 8 by 512 per MoE layer.

| layer | row norm, init | row norm, step 500 | relative weight change | row direction cosine over time | cross-row cosine, init to step 500 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.4466 | 0.4737 | 0.310 | 0.957 | 0.0179 to 0.0003 |
| 2 | 0.4524 | 0.4749 | 0.235 | 0.975 | 0.0095 to -0.0041 |
| 3 | 0.4595 | 0.4821 | 0.234 | 0.975 | 0.0057 to -0.0075 |
| 4 | 0.4527 | 0.4748 | 0.220 | 0.978 | 0.0058 to -0.0074 |
| 5 | 0.4480 | 0.4729 | 0.240 | 0.975 | -0.0014 to -0.0155 |
| 6 | 0.4574 | 0.4770 | 0.211 | 0.979 | 0.0151 to 0.0025 |
| 7 | 0.4493 | 0.4637 | 0.199 | 0.981 | 0.0008 to -0.0072 |

Calculated: each expert's gate row keeps 96 to 98 percent of its initial
direction while growing by 3 to 6 percent in norm. Cross-row cosine moves
toward zero, so the routing vectors become slightly more orthogonal. The gate
moved but did not restructure.

### 5.2 Expert parameter divergence

Measured on the full expert weights (1,474,560 values per expert), layers 1 to
7:

| layer | cross-expert cosine, init | cross-expert cosine, step 500 | expert norm, init to 500 | displacement norm | displacement cosine between experts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.000067 | 0.000120 | 24.29 to 24.96 | 5.47 | 0.0020 |
| 2 | 0.000073 | 0.000087 | 24.28 to 25.05 | 5.83 | 0.0001 |
| 3 | 0.000073 | 0.000113 | 24.29 to 25.13 | 6.06 | 0.0003 |
| 4 | 0.000372 | 0.000473 | 24.28 to 25.18 | 6.19 | 0.0011 |
| 5 | 0.000082 | -0.000007 | 24.28 to 25.26 | 6.45 | -0.0002 |
| 6 | -0.000220 | -0.000129 | 24.29 to 25.23 | 6.37 | 0.0010 |
| 7 | 0.000144 | 0.000192 | 24.29 to 25.20 | 6.33 | 0.0007 |

Two facts follow. First, experts stay mutually near-orthogonal in weight
space at both ends, so training did not collapse them into copies. Second, the
displacement vectors between step 0 and step 500 are themselves near-orthogonal
across experts (cosine about 0.001 to 0.002), so experts received visibly
different update directions even though their assignment counts are nearly
equal. Different gradients are not yet the same as different functions; the
next section tests the functions.

### 5.3 Expert output similarity

Measured per token on the same bounded validation set: mean cosine between
pairs of expert outputs, and the residual ratio
`mean_over_experts(||out_e - mean_out||) / ||mean_out||`, which is scale free
and falls when experts agree on a shared component.

| layer | output cosine, init to step 500 | residual ratio, init to step 500 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.1424 to 0.1855 | 2.6499 to 2.2181 |
| 2 | 0.1430 to 0.1799 | 2.6428 to 2.2414 |
| 3 | 0.1426 to 0.1926 | 2.6465 to 2.1563 |
| 4 | 0.1424 to 0.1931 | 2.6483 to 2.1427 |
| 5 | 0.1421 to 0.2117 | 2.6535 to 2.0104 |
| 6 | 0.1441 to 0.2640 | 2.6305 to 1.7734 |
| 7 | 0.1434 to 0.3391 | 2.6387 to 1.4970 |

Outputs became **more similar**, not more distinct, and the effect grows with
depth. The residual ratio falls by 16 percent in layer 1 and by 43 percent in
layer 7. This is the opposite of the signature expected from learned expert
specialization, which would push pairwise output similarity down and the
residual ratio up.

Caution: all experts receive the same input vector, so some shared component is
unavoidable, and cosine similarity is not a complete functional description.
The direction of change is still informative because init and step 500 are
measured on identical tokens.

### 5.4 Is routing conditioned on data source?

Measured per layer on three source-pure validation sets, 20,440 tokens each.
Divergence is Jensen-Shannon in bits between top-1 assignment distributions.
For 8 experts the ceiling (uniform against a point mass) is 0.7169 bits.

| layer | between-source JS, step 500 | within-source half JS | ratio | position-half JS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.00654 | 0.00273 | 2.40 | 0.00023 |
| 2 | 0.01065 | 0.00235 | 4.53 | 0.00119 |
| 3 | 0.00786 | 0.00177 | 4.44 | 0.00031 |
| 4 | 0.00932 | 0.00166 | 5.63 | 0.00027 |
| 5 | 0.01082 | 0.00265 | 4.08 | 0.00033 |
| 6 | 0.01377 | 0.00402 | 3.43 | 0.00027 |
| 7 | 0.01676 | 0.00511 | 3.28 | 0.00029 |

Aggregated: between-source divergence averages 0.01082 bits, which is 1.51
percent of the ceiling. Within-source split-half divergence averages 0.00290
bits, so the source effect is 2.4 to 5.6 times sampling-scale noise but tiny in
absolute terms. At initialization the same statistic averaged 0.03659 bits
(5.10 percent of ceiling) with a within-source baseline of 0.030 to 0.069
bits, i.e. unstructured variation.

The position control is decisive for one alternative explanation: splitting
each sequence at token 256 produces JS of 0.00009 to 0.00133 bits at step 500,
so routing is not driven by position. At init it was 0.004 to 0.046 bits.

**Interpretation (hypothesis, not measurement):** the trained router answers
some token-level signal that is shared across these three sources. Domain alone
does not select experts. A lexical or syntactic probe would be needed to
identify the signal, and it was not run.

### 5.5 Verdict

| claim | status | basis |
|---|---|---|
| balanced expert utilization | **measured, yes** | shares 10.6 to 14.1 percent, CV at most 0.073, no unused experts, aux 0.83 percent above floor |
| confident, distinct per-token routing | **measured, yes** | softmax entropy 0.61 to 0.87, mean max prob 0.35 to 0.58 |
| routing differs by data source | **measured, weak** | between-source JS 0.007 to 0.017 bits, 1.5 percent of ceiling, 2.4 to 5.6x noise |
| learned expert specialization | **not supported** | expert output cosine rises 0.14 to 0.19-0.34, residual ratio falls, weight-space cross-cosine stays near zero |

Balanced utilization is therefore confirmed, routing is confident and
non-positional, domain conditioning is detectable but negligible in size, and
no evidence of learned functional specialization was found in 500 updates.

## 9. Limitations and uncertainties

- One seed, one configuration, 500 updates, 16.35 million CE targets. Nothing
  here establishes long-run stability, convergence or final quality.
- 17 percent of one epoch. The curve is still falling at the stop, so the
  measured slope does not describe later behaviour.
- Training CE is one 64-sequence microbatch mean with dropout active;
  validation is the full split with dropout disabled. Their difference cannot
  be read as a generalization gap.
- The v3.1-L timer lacked explicit synchronization (about 2 percent
  consistency, not counter-grade).
- Router telemetry entropy is over top-2 assignment counts; the softmax
  entropy reported in section 4.3 comes from a different, bounded measurement.
- Specialization probes used 240 sequences total on validation data only. They
  are sufficient to reject strong specialization, not to prove its absence at
  a finer granularity.
- Source-level routing divergence is coarse. Token-level or feature-level
  structure was not identified, so the mechanism behind the confident routing
  remains a hypothesis.
- No GPU numerical resume parity test exists; structural resume is verified,
  numerical determinism after restore is not.
- The compute estimate uses 6ND and ignores attention terms.

## 10. Recommendations

Ranked by information gain per GPU-second. None of these was executed.

### Rank 1: B. Investigate expert specialization

- Expected information gain: decides whether the MoE layout is worth its 4x
  expert compute at this scale, and whether the 0.01 auxiliary weight or the
  routing objective should change. Current evidence says utilization is
  balanced but experts converge functionally.
- Main risks: probes may again be inconclusive; changing the routing objective
  or auxiliary weight changes a frozen architecture contract and needs explicit
  authorization. Cheap probes carry little technical risk.
- Required GPU work: none for the analysis-level probes already possible; one
  short bounded run only if a new objective is authorized.
- Architecture A unchanged for the diagnosis; any objective change would
  require a new freeze.

### Rank 2: A. Continue Dense-Combined training

- Expected information gain: measures whether the still-falling validation
  curve continues, and tests stability and numerical resume parity beyond 500
  updates.
- Main risks: cost scales linearly in GPU time and disk (1.57 GB per state);
  the improvement rate per update has already fallen about 10x; no new
  capability is unlocked at this scale.
- Required GPU work: one bounded run with a new wall-clock and disk budget.
- Architecture A unchanged.

### Rank 3: C. Revisit Sparse-MoE later

- Expected information gain: removes the dense workaround and cuts expert FLOPs
