## 4. Boundary exposure and attribution

Both runs produced the same two tight-boundary operands, at page distance 4 and
grid distance 4, and both were named directly by the tag registry:

| run | pointer | producer (observed) | phase | reads | OP_N | OP_T | fault |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | `0x7fb639a00000` | `?.mul` | forward | 2 | 1 | 1 | none |
| A | `0x7fb615200000` | `grad_of:torch._C._nn.linear` | backward | 1 | 0 | 1 | none |
| B | `0x7f2739400000` | `?.mul` | forward | 2 | 1 | 1 | none |
| B | `0x7f2714c00000` | `grad_of:torch._C._nn.linear` | backward | 1 | 0 | 1 | none |

Producer, phase and consumer roles are **observed**. Lifetime is **partial**: the
tag exists before the read and no reuse is recorded, but the two addresses fall
outside the retained history (16,383 intervals, 8 blocks with requested size
4,194,300), so the free state is unknown.

The block-size observation repeats: requests of 4,194,300 bytes are satisfied with
4,194,304-byte blocks, which is the 4-byte rounding gap.

## 6. What this does and does not establish

Established: the fault did not reproduce in three instrumented stock runs, and the
runs were not clean for lack of exposure. The full-precision first loss is
unchanged and identical to the reference expectation, so the instrumentation is
numerically transparent on this evidence.

Not established, and deliberately not claimed: that instrumentation suppresses the
fault. The uninstrumented stock configuration faulted in two of two earlier
attempts (PERF-5F A, PERF-5G A) against zero of three with instrumentation, which
is suggestive at best. A one-sided Fisher comparison of 2 of 2 against 0 of 3
gives p = 0.1, which does not support a suppression claim.

Also not established: which kernel instance over-reads when the fault does occur.
No solution IDs are logged in this build, and a fault page matching a tensor's page
is not proof of the faulting instruction.

## 8. Remaining uncertainty

1. Whether the three clean runs reflect suppression or a lower base rate under
   current conditions. The two facts cannot be separated without an uninstrumented
   control run.
2. Whether the earlier faults depended on an allocation neighbourhood that the
   instrumentation perturbs, for example through the extra 10 to 17 MB snapshot
   buffers.
3. The faulting instruction remains unobserved in every run.

## 9. Recommended next experiment

**Two uninstrumented stock runs with the same containment and no provenance at
all**, using the existing mode 0 harness, with the full-precision first loss
printed. This is the only cheap way to separate the two remaining explanations:

- if they fault, instrumentation suppression becomes the leading explanation and
  the next step is to strip instrumentation (tags only, no allocator history);
- if they are clean too, the fault's base rate has changed under current
  conditions and the PERF-5I, 5L and 5N attributions must be re-examined rather
  than carried forward.

Gates: display losses identical and first loss equal to `10.481635093688965`; the
run must show at least one tail-4 operand read to count as a meaningful control;
stop at the first fault.
