# v3.1-PERF-5N 2 MiB front-padding boundary control

Status 2026-10-27. One GPU execution, used. No production change, no checkpoint
change, no package change, no reset, no retry, no serialization, no push.

**Outcome: classification 4, fault elsewhere.**

The 2 MiB front pad was applied exactly as specified and is bitwise neutral, but
no product landed in the critical geometry: the closest one came 36,876 bytes
from a 2 MiB line. The run then faulted at a **stock** `(3075, 682)` bf16 GEMM
operand sitting at page distance 4 and grid distance 4, which is not any of the
padded products. That is a fault at another allocation, and it also exposes a
gap in the earlier attribution: the identical geometry appears on a stock tensor
in the two runs that were clean.

## 2. Implementation

Mode 4, additive to the harness:

```python
raw  = torch.empty(tensor_bytes + 2097152, dtype=torch.uint8)
view = raw[2097152:2097152 + tensor_bytes].view(dtype).view(shape)
torch.mul(a, b, out=view)
```

`PaddedMulFront2MiB` wraps the multiplication, `SwiGLU.forward` is replaced per
instance, `w3` is untouched and the product gradient is not padded. 2 MiB is a
multiple of both the page size and the block granularity, so a product that ends
4 bytes before a 2 MiB line keeps that distance while the allocation doubles and
the address shifts.

## 6. Fault attribution

Fault page `0x00007f505b600000`, 4 records, no reset. It lies inside **no**
padded product storage (0 of 2,622).

Two page-exact candidates:

| pointer | bytes | page_dist | grid_2mib_dist | kind | reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0x7f505ae00000` | 8,388,608 | 0 | 0 | stock, `(16384, 256)` dense activation | 212, outputs and operands |
| **`0x7f505b200000`** | **4,194,300** | **4** | **4** | **stock, `(3075, 682)` bf16** | **4**, at 17:52:09 |

The first ends exactly on the page, so its last element does not straddle and it
is not the fault geometry. The second is 4 bytes before a 2 MiB line, 2 MiB
aligned, and was read by both consumer shapes in the faulting second:

| Cdesc | transB | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `256 x 682` | `OP_T` | weight-gradient shaped, twice |
| `256 x 3075` | `OP_N` | forward shaped, twice |

It is not a padded product, and the product is provably padded in this arm, so
this tensor is a **SwiGLU backward derivative**: the gradient of the product with
respect to either `silu(w1(x))` or `w2(x)`. The log cannot say which of the two,
and it cannot separate those descriptors from the `w3` forward and weight-gradient
pair, which produce the same shapes.

## 7. What this does to the series

Every tight-boundary tensor found so far is the same object: a 2 MiB aligned
`(M, 682)` bf16 GEMM operand ending 4 bytes before a 2 MiB line.

| run | arm | tail-4 tensor | read | faulted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PERF-5I | product stock, gradient padded | `0x7ff321000000` | 2 | yes |
| PERF-5J | product back-padded | `0x7fdf79800000` | 1 | **no** |
| PERF-5K | replication | `0x7f594be00000` | 1 | **no** |
| PERF-5L | product stock, gradient padded | `0x7fcccde00000` | 2 | yes |
| PERF-5N | product front-padded 2 MiB | `0x7f505b200000` | 4 | yes |

All five are 2 MiB aligned with `page_dist` 4 and `grid_2mib_dist` 4. Geometry
alone therefore does not separate the faulting runs from the clean ones, and in
5J and 5K that geometry was on a **stock** tensor while the products were padded.

That has two consequences for the campaign record:

1. The earlier claim that padding the product removes the fault is weaker than it
   looked: in the two clean runs a stock `(M, 682)` operand with the same tight
   geometry was read and did not fault.
2. The attribution of 5I and 5L to "the product" is not unique, because the same
   descriptor pair is produced by the derivative path. 5N proves the derivative
   can be the faulting tensor; it cannot retroactively decide 5I and 5L.

## 8. Interpretation

| class | verdict |
|---|---|
| 1, critical exposure with fault at the product boundary | no, the product's geometry never occurred |
| 2, critical exposure with clean completion | no |
| 3, valid run without critical exposure | true of the product, but a fault occurred |
| 4, fault elsewhere | **yes** |
| 5, invalid | no, correctness, isolation and descriptors all pass |

What is established: the fault can occur at a stock `(M, 682)` bf16 operand that
is not the SwiGLU product, at the tight 2 MiB-aligned 4-byte tail, while the
product is padded and far from any boundary.

What is not established: which tensor of that family faulted, whether the mapping
beyond the logical end was open or closed in any individual case, and therefore
whether the trigger is the over-read, the specific kernel, or the address layout.
Reporting mapping accessibility remains **unknown**.

## 9. Remaining uncertainty

The series now contains five tight-boundary tensors of the same shape with three
faults and two clean reads, all at the same descriptor-level geometry. Something
outside the descriptors decides the outcome: the kernel actually selected, the
surrounding mapping state, or which of the several `(M, 682)` tensors it was. The
records cannot separate these, and the current evidence does not justify naming
the product, or any single tensor, as the trigger.

## 10. Recommended next experiment

Two options, in order of information per run:

1. **Pad the derivative.** Extend `PaddedMul.backward` so the gradients it
   returns to `silu(w1(x))` and `w2(x)` land in padded storage, leaving the
   product stock. If the fault moves again, the trigger is any unprotected
   `(M, 682)` operand and per-tensor padding is a whack-a-mole; if it disappears,
   this particular consumer is implicated.
2. **Force the product's geometry.** Keep the 2 MiB pad but choose the front
   offset from the observed base, with a bounded retry over allocation sizes,
   until a product actually lands at `page_dist 4` and `grid_2mib_dist 4`, then
   require the census to confirm it before interpreting. That completes the
   comparison 5N was meant to make.

Until then the campaign should not claim that padding the product prevents the
fault.
